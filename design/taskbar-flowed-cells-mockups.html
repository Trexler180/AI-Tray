Named cells, flowed across two rows

Two changes to names inside the cells: the CX/CL tag is gone — the fill colour was already saying it — and rows are no longer tied to a provider. Dropping the tag is what makes the second change possible: once nothing labels a row, a row doesn't have to belong to one side, so accounts can flow across both rows and every cell gets a fair share of the width. Removing the tag also takes 21 px off the width, which is the widest the widget ever needed to be.

The packing rule: lay the accounts out in reading order — Codex first, then Claude — and split them ceil(n/2) on the top row, the rest below. So 1 Codex + 2 Claude puts the Codex and the first Claude on top and the second Claude underneath, rather than leaving one lonely stretched Codex cell above a row of two. With a single account on a row, that cell spans the full width.

The case you asked about

1 Codex, 2 Claude, at 3.2×, three ways. The middle one is the rule above; the others are what it is avoiding. Change the counts in the controls and all three update.

Treatments

All three are the same widget — named cells, pace marks, weekly tucked under, no tag. They differ only in how accounts are assigned to the two rows, which is the whole question once a row is no longer owned by a provider.

Every combination

The recommended treatment at 1.9×, for every count from 1 to 3 on each side — including the lopsided ones and the single-provider row. This is the proof that it holds together at any mix, not just the symmetric ones.

Measured heights

From the rendered DOM. The button is 32 px; over that overflows, 29–32 fits with no breathing room. Height is constant at every combination — it is always two rows — and only the width and the per-cell share change.
