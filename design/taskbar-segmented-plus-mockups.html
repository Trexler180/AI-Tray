Segmented rail, with the pace marks, labels and a weekly bar

Four things at once: the segmented rail (one cell per account), the pace mark the one-rail-per-account face had, text labels over the cells, and a thinner weekly bar under each provider's 5-hour row. That is five stacked elements per provider inside a 32 px button, so the real question is not whether it looks good — it does — but whether it fits. Every card below prints its own measured height, taken from the DOM after render, so the verdict is arithmetic rather than opinion.

The arithmetic, as measured: label row 7.5px (account names) or 6.5px (initials) + 5-hour rail 4px + weekly rail 2px + two inner gaps 2px = 15.5px per provider. Two providers plus the gap between them is 34px — two pixels over a 32 px button. The label row is what breaks it: cut those two rows to initials and tuck the weekly bar flush, and the same four elements land at 31px. Treatments 03–05 are the three ways to pay for it.

Anatomy

One provider block at 5.2×, so the parts are separable. At real size this whole block is 14 px tall — the height of this sentence's lowercase letters.

Label row
One per cell, centred on it. Short account names when the cells are wide enough to hold them, initials when they aren't — the widget picks per render.
5-hour cell
One account's session window. Fill is what's spent; the cell is its own track, so accounts never share a scale.
Pace mark
How far through the window the clock is. Fill behind the mark means you're inside your pace; past it means you'll run out early.
Weekly bar
The same accounts, same order, half the height and dimmed — present for the glance, never competing with the session row.

What I'd take from this: the combination is worth having. The weekly bar costs 3 px and answers a question the session row can't, and the pace marks survive being cut into cells far better than I expected — even at compact width, where a cell is under 20 px. What does not survive is two rows of account names: that is the element that puts 01 over the button, and the honest reading of the badge is that the literal request needs one concession. 03 (tucked, initials only) is the cheapest one — it keeps all four elements at 31 px and spends a gap and one point of type size. 05 (shared label row) is the one I would actually build, because when both providers have the same account names — the common case — the second label row was never carrying new information; it fits with room to spare and keeps names readable. 04 (side by side) is the escape hatch if width is cheaper than height on your taskbar, which it usually is not.
