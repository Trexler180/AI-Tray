Taskbar widget — ten ways to show the meters

The app currently gets one 16 px tray icon and everything else lives behind a click. These are alternatives that put the numbers on the taskbar the way the Windows weather widget does: a persistent button, always readable, no click required. Every mockup below is real markup at real size — the strip is a 48 px Windows 11 taskbar, and each widget is 32 px tall, exactly what a taskbar button gets.

THE BUDGET
About 32×140 px. The weather widget spends it on an icon and two numbers. Four quota windows do not fit — every concept below decides what to drop.
READ TIME
A taskbar glance is well under a second and unfocused. Shape and color survive that; digits mostly don't.
STILLNESS
It's on screen all day. Anything that changes often in the corner of your eye becomes a nuisance — turn on Live to see which ones twitch.
BOTH THEMES
Light taskbars are common and the app has never had to render on one. Each concept is shown on #202020 and #f3f3f3.

What the hover reveals

Whichever face wins, the widget still needs the full picture one hover away — the weather widget's own trick. This is the flyout: all four windows, their pace marks, and today's estimated value. The existing popover stays for the click.

HOVER STATE

My read: 03 Stacked hairlines is the one I would build. It shows all four windows, needs no digits to be read, holds still, and the single countdown answers the only question a glance ever asks — how long until this clears? 05 Tightest-first is the strong runner-up and the better default for anyone who only cares when something is nearly gone. 02 Rings and 10 Tray text are the quiet options if the taskbar is crowded.
