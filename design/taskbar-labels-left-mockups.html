Labels to the left, and the bars get their pixels back

Moving the account labels out of a row above the bars and into a column beside them doesn't just tidy the layout — it hands the height back. The label rows were costing 15 px of a 32 px button; spend that on the bars instead and the 5-hour rail goes from 4 px to 8–11 px, which is tall enough to set the account name inside its own cell. Same four elements as before, none of them dropped. Every card measures its own height from the DOM, so the badges are arithmetic, not opinion.

Where the 15 px goes: with labels above, a provider cost 7.5 + 4 + 2 + gaps = 15.5px and two of them overflowed at 34px. With the tag on the left, a provider is just bar + gap + weekly. Solve 2 × (b + 2 + b/2) + 3 = 32 and you get b = 8px — double the old bar, with the weekly at 4 px instead of 2. Stop letting the weekly scale with the bar (pin it at 3 px — it moves slowly enough) and tuck it flush, and the 5-hour bar reaches 10px at 31px total, which is where names fit inside the cells.

You were right that this is the better direction. Labels above cost height twice — once per provider — while a label to the left costs width once, and width is the axis a taskbar can actually spare. The measured result is that every element from the last page survives and the bars roughly double. 03 (names inside the cells) is the one I'd build: at 10 px the cell carries its own name, so the label column shrinks to a 15 px provider tag and nothing sits above anything. 01 is the conservative version if 6.5 px type inside a bar feels like a step too far, and 04 (rows by window) is the interesting outsider — it drops to two rails by letting colour carry the provider, which buys the tallest bars on the page. The one honest limit: at 3+3 on compact width a cell is under 20 px and the inside-name falls back to an initial, so 03 quietly becomes 01 for people with many accounts.
