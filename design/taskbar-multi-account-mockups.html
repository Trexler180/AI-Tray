Stacked lines, many accounts

The rail is the vocabulary worth keeping: a track, a fill, a pace mark, read without digits. The question is what happens when there are six of them. Two accounts fit the way two providers did; six do not, so each concept below either folds accounts into one rail, spends time (rotation, trails, reset order), or spends a gesture (hover, click). Every mockup is real markup at real size, and the interactive ones actually work — hover 06, click 07.

One assumption, stated: the app models multiple accounts for Claude only (ClaudeAccountUsage, one per config directory); CodexUsage is a single account today. These mockups assume Codex gains the same shape, because a design that only stacks on one side will not survive it later.
THE PIXEL WALL
Six 4 px rails plus gaps is ~44 px in a 32 px button. Four is the honest maximum for one-rail-per-account; past that something has to give.
WHAT MOTION COSTS
Rotation buys unlimited accounts and spends the ability to glance — the answer is only right one second in four. Turn Motion off to see the still frame.
WHAT A GESTURE COSTS
Hover and click are free of pixels and free of noise, but they are not a glance. They belong under a face that already works at rest.

What I'd actually build

Not one of these — three of them, layered, so the widget answers at every distance without ever moving on its own. Rest is still, hover is complete, click is a deliberate change of subject.

AT REST — 03 WORST-OF WITH TICKS
Two rails, one per provider, fill = the worst account, with a tick per account on the same rail. Constant height at any account count, still reads as the two-rail widget, and the tick spread answers "is this one account or all of them?" without a label.
ON HOVER — 06 EXPAND
The full list: one labelled rail per account per window, pace marks intact, resets on the right. Nothing is hidden from the person who asks, and asking costs no taskbar pixels.
ON CLICK — 07 PIN
Click cycles which account the rails follow and pins it, with the initial shown in a chip. For the common case — one account you actually work in — the widget becomes single-account and quiet again.
DELIBERATELY NOT — 05 ROTATION
It solves the count problem completely and it is the one thing you cannot un-see in the corner of your eye all day. Worth mocking to be sure; not worth shipping. 08 trails is the temporal idea I would keep, since it moves only as fast as the usage does.

The one open question is what the worst-of rail should do when two accounts are both near the top — the tick marks pile up at the same end and the spread stops reading. 10 (top-two plus "+2") is the fallback for that case, which is an argument for making it the overflow behaviour of 03 rather than a separate face.
